# Five-shop pilot field kit

Prepared for the lead and field collaborator. This is a blank operational template, not evidence that any shop has agreed, and not an agreement on compensation, IP, or access.

## Before visits

Agree one Hyderabad neighborhood, 2–3 shop categories, responsible interviewer, weekly visit availability, approved contact-storage location, and written collaboration terms. Shortlist 15–20 shops; interview 10–15 and seek five willing participants. Do not promise sales, launch dates, free lifetime use, or a price.

Suggested pitch: “We’re testing a simple offer page for local shops. Your shop gets a QR code customers can scan without installing an app. You can update offers from your phone. We’re inviting a few owners to try it and tell us what works. We can’t promise increased sales.” Adapt to Telugu/Hindi/local speech.

Ask what would make customers scan/return, what would make this worth paying for, and what monthly amount seems realistic. Record negative reactions as carefully as positive ones. Do not photograph a person, record voice, or collect unnecessary personal data without permission.

## Onboarding checklist

- Owner explicitly consents to the pilot and follow-up; support contact is agreed.
- Operator verifies control of the business and provisions only the required role; never share an admin account.
- Shop name, address, slug, and QR destination are checked together. Printed QRs use the permanent HTTPS domain.
- Customer scans on an actual low-cost Android phone, without signing in.
- Owner creates a text draft, checks price/quantity/end date, publishes, and confirms the customer page changes.
- Owner edits and withdraws an offer; the customer page reflects both changes.
- Photo/voice processing is explained as AI assistance requiring owner review. Explain what is stored and the retention/deletion policy.
- Observe weak network behavior, failed uploads, retry, and time to publish; manual text remains available.

| Willingness to pay (target 2/5) | Owner statement with date, conditions, proposed amount; no charge/commitment | | |

| Defects, drop-off, objections | Severity, reproduction, anonymized notes | | |

End each week with top findings, defects to fix, the responsible person, and next visit date. Escalate false offers, privacy complaints, and security concerns promptly to the lead.

## End-of-pilot decision

Review repeat owner use, value to customers, willingness to pay, support burden, and actual costs. Record continue/change/stop with supporting evidence. Expanding to 20–30 shops in months 4–5 is conditional; no numerical hypothesis guarantees success. Do not describe unperformed visits, consent, or a pilot outcome as complete.
